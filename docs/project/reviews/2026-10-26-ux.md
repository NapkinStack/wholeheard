# Wholeheard — UX/UI audit of the user-facing surface

Date: 2026-10-26 · Scope: `modules/shell` (window, small window, tray, notifications, words) · Read-only.
Line numbers are those of commit 66b1ff7: `main.rs` has moved by some fifty lines since, `window.js` by some twenty after line 1600; the content is unchanged (the verifier of #445).

**Evidence tags**
- *code*: seen in the code (file:line).
- *board*: seen on an accepted board (D11 or D34).
- *shot*: seen on an e2e screenshot that already exists from another worktree's run
  (the shell e2e's own screenshots of a worktree, made with stand-in data). I opened these files only; I ran nothing.

**Decisions I did not reopen** (cycle 03/04): small window first, one window at a time, back to rest at the stop (D34/D35); the page never follows the line being played (D26, "faire bouger le focus est un peu contre nature"); the minutes' model is saved only on *Enregistrer* (D25); the shortcut belongs to the desktop under Linux (D20); online transcription, recording by itself, correcting the transcript, the device offer and export are all *Later*; Ollama is never installed by Wholeheard (D37).

When a proposal departs from an accepted board, the text says so.

---

## Verdict in five lines

1. The bones are good: the boards are built faithfully, the eight states are mostly handled, failures are plain and actionable, and the privacy promise is explicit (board 5).
2. The biggest gap against "high-end and fluid" is that **a meeting has no name**. Rows and pages are titled by a date (`window.js:72-78`, *shot* 1-sessions), where every board drew a title. The list cannot be scanned.
3. Next: **the result is hard to take away** (no copy of the minutes), **too many notifications** per meeting, and **the success notification opens a file manager** instead of the meeting.
4. Some English technical strings leak into the French UI (`main.rs` `Err("…")` → `not_saved`), against the module's own rule.
5. The French is correct but often **translated-sounding and long** ("ce qui est là est vrai quand même", "pour qui cherche sa cause", "accordé par le bureau"). A wording pass is cheap and would lift the feel the most.

## 8. Visual polish

- **Icons** are Unicode glyphs ("›", "⤢", "✓", "→", "×"). They render differently by font and system, and "⤢" is missing from some fonts. Replace them with five inline SVGs from Lucide (ISC licence): chevron-right, maximize-2, minimize-2, check, arrow-right. Inline means no dependency. (*code* `window.js:333,1529,202,1456`)
- **Typography**: `system-ui`, the same as the boards. The page title is the date in 28 px bold, which reads as data, not as a title (3.1 fixes the cause). The monospace makers ("Écrit par …") are as on board 2 and fine.
- **Colour**: the tokens match the boards in light and dark. The contrast pairs checked by eye are fine (white on `#2253CF`, `#1E2129` on `#8AB0FF`). There is no automated contrast check: a small test over the token pairs would cover it (ux.md: "whatever can be automated").
- **Motion**: three animations (the beat, the making bar, the chevron), all of which respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. For "playful" without over-engineering: a short fade or slide when the small window switches between rest and live, and a one-time 200 ms "pop" on a chip that turns from *Compte rendu…* to *Compte rendu*. Nothing more.
- **Spacing**: consistent (gaps of 10/12/22/26). The meeting header's chips align to the bottom of the title block, so they float (*shot* 2-session). Align them to the date's baseline, or put them under the title.

## 9. Showing the cost of an AI call

*Read with the market review, §6, which checked it at the source: OpenRouter returns `usage.cost` in every response, `usage: {include: true}` deprecated and without effect; its page says "cost in credits" — dollars, an assumption. The two reports differ on tokens: this one would never show them, the market review would show them beside the cost — the decider's to settle at the next framing.*

**Facts**
- Nothing records a cost or token usage today. `grep usage|cost` over `modules/summary` and `contracts/minutes|summarising` finds nothing. The `minutes/v1` front matter holds `made`, `provider`, `model` and `context` only.
- Online transcription is *Later* (narrowed out of D20). Today the cost question concerns the minutes alone.
- Most OpenAI-compatible APIs return `usage` as token counts, not a price.

**Assumption, to verify before building**: OpenRouter can return the call's price in its response (`usage.cost`, through its "usage accounting"; at some point this needed `usage: {include: true}` in the request). It also has a generation lookup endpoint that returns a total cost. I did not check the current API in this session.

**Recommendation**
1. **Show it only when the provider itself reports a price.** Never estimate it from a price table we would have to maintain: that is over-engineering, and it is wrong whenever prices change. Never show tokens to the user (jargon). If needed, they can go in the log and in developer mode.
2. **Where**: on the minutes' maker line, where the reader already looks for "who made this":
   `Écrit par Claude Sonnet, via openrouter.ai · mar. 13 oct. à 12:21 · 0,012 $`
   In the currency the provider reports, formatted with `Intl.NumberFormat` (`style: "currency"`), and in `--soft`, never highlighted.
3. **Optionally**, one cumulative line in Settings under the online provider: "Ce mois-ci : 0,31 $ pour 24 comptes rendus" (a sum over the front matters, shell only). Do this only if the decider wants it; it is not needed to decide anything.
4. **Not** in the agreement page (board 5): an estimate before the call is exactly the price table we refuse to keep. The agreement already says how many words leave, which is the honest proxy.
5. **Cost of the change**: a contract change. Add an optional `cost` (plus `currency`) to the `minutes/v1` front matter, additive (expand first, readers ignore an unknown key, then the producer writes it). It touches the summary (request and response) and the shell (display), so it goes through the §4 process. Size M.
