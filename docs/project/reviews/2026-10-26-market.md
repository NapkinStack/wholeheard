# Wholeheard — market review, 2026-10-26

Scope: botless desktop meeting note-takers and the platform-native alternatives, as of
2026-10-26. Builds on `docs/project/discovery.md` §2 and §5 (research of 2026-10-12) and on the
prior-art table of PDR-0001 (2026-10-19); repeats neither, says where they changed.

**Method and markers.** Web search and page fetch, all pages read **2026-10-26** unless stated.
*Vendor* = a company's page about itself. *Secondary* = a review, comparison or aggregator,
often written by a competitor (Laxis, Sonix, tl;dv, Meetily, Spokenly, Voibe, LumeVoice publish
"X pricing" pages about their rivals) — figures from them are marked and may be stale.
*Unverified* = found in one secondary source only. No product was installed or run for this
review: every UX claim is from documentation, not observation.

## 2. Trends 2025–2026

1. **Botless is the new default, not a niche.** Every cloud leader above ships a desktop or
   extension capture. Consequence: Wholeheard's pitch cannot lead with "no bot"; it must lead
   with **where the meeting goes** (nowhere) and **what it costs** (once, or nothing).
2. **Consent moved from the bot to the defaults.** Granola's lawsuit is about botless capture
   with notice off by default; Notion now interposes a consent step; Apple's OS plays an audio
   notice; Meet notifies all participants. Wholeheard's start reminder (charter C7) is aligned;
   a one-click "copy the announcement" is the cheap upgrade (§4).
3. **On-device got good and cheap.** SpeechAnalyzer (macOS 26) beat Whisper Small on English
   LibriSpeech (2.12 % vs 3.74 % WER) and ran ~3× faster in a vendor benchmark; no French
   figure exists publicly ([mjtsai, 2026-07-22](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2026/07/22/whisper-and-speechanalyzer/), *secondary*). Parakeet and Qwen
   models appear in Meetily/OpenWhispr. Local is commodity — dozens of HN projects (§0).
4. **Templates by meeting type** are universal (Granola 1:1/stand-up/…, Notion by meeting
   type, Jamie, Summit AI Notes, Meetily Pro, OpenWhispr "Sync to template").
5. **Ask-your-notes / chat across meetings + MCP** is the leaders' 2026 battleground
   (Granola Chat rebuild 2026-05 and MCP 2026-04; Jamie MCP; Anarlog local MCP; Otter quotas).
   Out of Wholeheard's charter.
6. **Traceability of AI claims**: Granola's magnifier, Notion's citations. Reason: action-item
   accuracy reported at 62–87 % and invented facts in up to 37 % of generated summaries
   (Kirstein et al. 2024, *secondary* via [Saner](https://blog.saner.ai/ai-note-taking-statistics/), not read at source).
7. **Speaker naming is the #1 functional complaint** of local tools (HN Hyprnote launch;
   MacWhisper groups remote participants; Granola falls back without Zoom). Leaders solve it
   with conferencing metadata (Zoom active speaker) or diarization (Meetily Pro, OpenWhispr);
   Anarlog Free offers **manual labelling**.
8. **Live notes / pre-meeting briefs** (Granola Briefs 2026-07, Fireflies Live Assist) —
   calendar-driven, cloud. Out of scope.
9. **Cost transparency is absent from meeting tools.** Metering exists only as quotas
   (minutes, credits, query counts); see §5.
10. **Price pressure on subscriptions; lifetime licences return** for local tools: MacWhisper
    €59, Summit AI Notes $149.99, Superwhisper $249.99 (*secondary*). Wholeheard's €49–69 sits
    at the low end — credible.

## 4. Differentiators Wholeheard can credibly own (without over-engineering)

1. **The meeting never leaves, and the user can see it didn't — per meeting.** Granola deletes
   audio but keeps notes in its cloud, shares by link by default, trains by default and is
   sued; Notion sends audio to three sub-processors. A per-minutes **"receipt"**: which
   provider ran each step, what left the machine, tokens, cost. Nobody shows this (§5).
2. **Cost of each AI call, in euros, on the minutes.** Zero competition in meeting tools; the
   data is free from OpenRouter (`usage.cost`, token counts, in every response).
3. **Verify by listening.** Wholeheard keeps the audio as recorded (ADR-0004); Granola cannot.
   A citation from each decision/action to the transcript **and the audio at that second** turns
   the known weakness (≈1 word in 4 missed on a lively meeting) into a checkable one.
4. **First-class Linux, from the distribution's own repositories.** Anarlog: beta, no rpm;
   Meetily: none; OpenWhispr: packages, but a freemium cloud company. Copr + PPA with the system
   updater is unmatched in this category.
5. **One price, once, and nothing on Linux.** €49–69 undercuts Summit AI Notes' lifetime
   ($149.99) and every subscription (Granola $168/yr, Jamie Pro €468/yr).
6. **French first.** Granola and Summit AI Notes have no French UI; the leaders' French wording
   is inconsistent (§6). A minutes layout that reads like a French *compte rendu* is cheap.

What it should **not** try to own: diarization quality, live AI assistance, cross-meeting chat,
team workspaces, CRM — each is a leader's core and a large build.

**Recommended shape for Wholeheard** (decision for the decider, not a fact):
- Where: one line under the minutes' title, and in a details panel ("receipt") per step.
- Wording FR: *« Compte rendu : 0,0042 € — OpenRouter, mistral-small, 12 480 jetons »*;
  local: *« Sur cette machine — 0 € »*; provider without a cost field:
  *« Coût non communiqué par le service — 12 480 jetons »*. EN: *"Minutes: €0.0042 —
  OpenRouter, …, 12,480 tokens"*, *"On this machine — free"*, *"Cost not reported by the service"*.
- Currency: OpenRouter reports **US dollars** (credits are USD) — show `$` as reported, or
  convert with a stated rate; never a silent conversion. *Assumption:* `usage.cost` is in USD
  credits — the docs page read says "total charged to your account" without naming the unit.
- Before the call: an estimate only when it is honest (tokens of the transcript × the model's
  listed price from OpenRouter's `/models`); otherwise show nothing rather than a guess.
- Never a running meter during recording (it creates anxiety, Cline's context is different).
